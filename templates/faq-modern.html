1. What is Phishing? ⌄

Phishing is a cybercrime technique where attackers impersonate legitimate organizations to trick you into revealing sensitive information. Common methods include:

  • Fake emails requesting password resets or account verification
  • Fraudulent login pages mimicking banks or social media
  • Malicious links and attachments in emails or messages
  • SMS messages (smishing) claiming urgent account action needed

Always verify sender addresses, hover over links before clicking, and never click links in unexpected emails.

2. How Can I Identify a Phishing Email? ⌄

Look for these red flags in emails:

  • Generic greetings: "Dear Customer" instead of your name
  • Urgent language: "Verify immediately" or "Account will be closed"
  • Suspicious sender: Check the full email address, not just display name
  • Spelling/grammar errors: Professional companies rarely have mistakes
  • Requests for sensitive info: Banks never ask for passwords via email
  • Suspicious links: Hover to see actual URL; it should match company domain
  • Unexpected attachments: Especially .exe, .zip, or macro-enabled files

Best practice: When in doubt, contact the company directly using a number from their official website.

3. What is URL/Link Spoofing and How Do I Detect It? ⌄

URL spoofing is when attackers disguise malicious links to look legitimate. Detection tips:

  • Hover before clicking: Move your mouse over links to see the actual URL
  • Check the domain: amazon.com ≠ amaz0n.com or amazon-verify.com
  • Look for HTTPS: Legitimate sites use HTTPS (padlock icon), not HTTP
  • Shortened URLs: Expand short links to see where they go
  • IP addresses: Links with numbers instead of domain names are suspicious
  • Unusual TLDs: .tk, .ml, or other uncommon extensions are risky

Use our URL Scanner: Paste any suspicious link to get a risk analysis.

4. What is QR Code Phishing and How Do I Stay Safe? ⌄

QR code phishing (QRishing) uses fake QR codes to direct you to malicious websites. Safety measures:

  • Be cautious of unexpected QR codes: Don't scan from emails, texts, or strangers
  • Check the source: Verify the QR code came from a trusted location
  • Preview before opening: Many phones show the URL before opening
  • Look for signs of tampering: Stickers over original codes are suspicious
  • Use our QR Scanner: We decode and analyze QR codes safely

Always verify decoded URLs before following them, especially for sensitive transactions.

5. Is it Safe to Share OTPs, CVV, and Other Sensitive Information? ⌄

NEVER share these with anyone:

  • OTPs (One-Time Passwords)
  • CVV/CVC (Card security codes)
  • PINs (Personal Identification Numbers)
  • Password or Security answers
  • Aadhar, PAN, or other government IDs
  • Bank account or card numbers (except secure payment gateways)

Remember: Legitimate organizations (banks, government, companies) will NEVER ask for sensitive information via:

  • Email or text message
  • Phone calls
  • Social media
  • Chat applications

If someone requests this information, it's a scam. Report it immediately.

6. What is Social Engineering and How Do I Protect Myself? ⌄

Social engineering is psychological manipulation to trick you into revealing confidential information or compromising security. Common tactics:

  • Authority: Attackers pose as IT support, police, or government officials
  • Urgency: Creating time pressure ("Act now or account will be closed")
  • Fear: Threats of legal action or account suspension
  • Trust: Building rapport with victims before requesting information
  • Reciprocity: Offering free gifts or assistance to gain favor

Protection strategies:

  • Verify caller identity through official channels
  • Never give information to unexpected callers
  • Be skeptical of unsolicited offers
  • Discuss suspicious requests with colleagues or family
  • Hang up and call back official numbers to verify
7. How Do I Report Cyber Scams and Phishing? ⌄

Report to multiple authorities to maximize impact:

  • Your Bank/Service Provider: Contact immediately to freeze accounts or reverse transactions
  • Cybercrime Units: Local police cybercrime cell or online complaint portal
  • FBI (International): Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3): ic3.gov
  • Email Provider: Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo have built-in reporting features
  • Our Platform: Report here for threat intelligence and community protection
  • Reserve Bank (India): Central Fraud Management System (CFMS)
  • Tech Platforms: Report phishing on Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, etc.

What to provide: Screenshots, email headers, links, transaction details, and timeline of events.

8. What are Best Practices to Stay Safe Online? ⌄

Essential security habits:

  • Use strong passwords: Mix uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Use 12+ characters
  • Enable 2FA/MFA: Add an extra layer of protection on important accounts
  • Update software: Keep OS, browsers, and applications up-to-date
  • Use antivirus: Install reputable antivirus and malware protection
  • Backup data: Regular backups protect against ransomware
  • Use VPN: Encrypt traffic on public WiFi
  • Review statements: Check bank and credit card statements regularly
  • Monitor credit: Watch for unauthorized accounts or loans
  • Be skeptical: Think before clicking, always verify requests
  • Use password manager: Securely store and generate unique passwords

Remember: Stay vigilant. If something feels wrong, it probably is.
